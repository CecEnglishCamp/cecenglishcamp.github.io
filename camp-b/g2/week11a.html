2 Reading · 오늘의 본문
The Iron Heel · Jack London · 1908
In the story, a small group of very rich men began to join together. They owned the banks, the factories and the newspapers. They called themselves protectors of order, but they wanted power for themselves. Ernest warned that they would not give up their wealth peacefully. Later, people gave this group a name: the Iron Heel. It was a heavy foot pressing down on the working people.
B2 · 128 words avg 25.6 w/s
The title image arrives as a prophecy. Ernest warns that the great owners, once their interests converge, will cease to compete and begin to govern, and that a government of owners will answer resistance with force. London gives that coalition the name of the Iron Heel, a metaphor for power that does not argue but presses. The warning is placed early so that later events read as fulfillment, and the reader watches a prediction harden into history.
C1 · 170 words avg 34.0 w/s
